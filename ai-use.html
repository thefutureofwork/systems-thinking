AI Use Guide

What you may and may not use AI for in EAP 101, assignment by assignment, from Week 1 to Week 14.

The one rule

You may use AI in this course. You may not use AI to do the thinking the assignment exists to build.

The test: if AI performs the step being assessed, that is a breach of academic integrity. If AI supports you around that step, it is a tool. Every assignment below tells you which is which.

Where this guide is more specific than the general policy in your syllabus, this guide applies, because it is written for these tasks. If a use is not listed and you are unsure, ask me before you submit.

01

Three zones

Green · use freely, no disclosure needed

  • Looking up a word, a term, or a name you heard or read.
  • Asking for a definition or a simpler explanation of a course concept, after you have done the reading or listening.
  • Quizzing yourself: ask AI to test you on s / o links, R / B loops, the four Iceberg levels, or the archetype names.
  • Checking spelling, grammar, and punctuation in text you have already written yourself.
  • Formatting an APA reference for a source you are holding and have actually read.
  • Pronunciation practice and speaking rehearsal before a seminar.

Amber · allowed, but write an AI Use Statement

  • Widening a list of ideas after you have written your own.
  • Asking AI to critique your own draft: is my research question specific, is this question leading, is this paragraph clear.
  • Asking AI to restate a hard passage in simpler English, which you then check against the original text.
  • Using an AI tool to help you find sources, which you then open and read yourself.
  • Auto-captions or auto-transcription that you then correct yourself.
  • Routine media processing in editing: noise reduction, stabilization, trimming.

Red · never, in any EAP 101 assignment

  • Submitting AI-generated sentences as your own writing, in any language, at any length.
  • Having AI produce your map, your causal loop, your graph, your Iceberg, or your labels.
  • Asking AI for citations, page numbers, or quotations, or treating it as a source of facts. It invents them.
  • Summarizing a reading, talk, or video you did not read or listen to yourself.
  • Writing any part of a reflection. Your reflections are about your life and your change.
  • Generating, altering, or voicing interview content, or presenting an unchecked AI transcript as your interviewee’s words.
  • Cloning a voice, or using a text-to-speech voice in place of your own narration.
  • Reading an AI-written script aloud in a seminar, or using AI during any graded seminar or in-class activity.
02

The AI Use Statement

This is the only disclosure format accepted in EAP 101. Four lines, at the end of the file you submit. No marks are deducted for honest amber-zone use. Marks are lost, and integrity procedures start, for use you hide.

AI Use Statement
1. Tool and version:
2. What I asked it to do:
3. What I did with the output:
4. What I checked or changed:

When one is needed. Green-zone use never requires a statement. Any amber-zone use does.

That single rule governs the Statement needed? column in every table below, so “No, if that is all you used” means you stayed in the green zone.

Where it goes: at the end of the same document you upload to your Student Folder. For the Video Project, save it as a short text file beside your video in Student Folder → Video Project, and credit any AI-generated visual on screen.

If you quote AI output: cite it in APA, for example OpenAI. (2027). ChatGPT (version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/chat

If you are not sure which zone you were in, write the statement. It costs you nothing.

Tasks not in the tables

Three standing rules, so there are no gaps.

  1. Anything marked “In class” is AI-free. That covers the four seminar-skills practices (gaining the floor, building on peers’ ideas, asking for clarification, challenging assumptions respectfully), the Constellations of Care mapping in Weeks 1 and 9, the opportunity-loop case in Week 6, and the accessibility redesign in Week 10.
  2. “Nothing to upload” does not mean “no rules”. The Week 1 toolkit reading, the Week 8 start on your bibliography, and your Week 9 office meeting all follow the three zones.
  3. If a use is not listed anywhere, ask me before you submit. Not after.

Sources, bibliography, and interview planning

The citation rule, stated once. AI invents sources. It produces real-looking authors, titles, journals, and page numbers for work that does not exist.

Any reference in your work that I cannot open is treated as a fabricated source, whatever its origin. Find your sources in the Library databases and read them before you cite them. Using a tool to help you find a source is amber: opening and reading it is still your job.

End-of-course reflection

Week 14 · Fri May 7, 23:59 · Student Folder → Week 14. Zero AI. No drafting, no rewriting, no polishing, no translating whole sentences. This is the one place where even a grammar check is out.

300–400 words revisiting the three habits you chose in Week 1. Write it yourself, imperfect English and all.

Why this one is stricter: this is the only piece of work in the course whose entire value is that it is yours. An AI-smoothed reflection tells me nothing about what changed in you over fourteen weeks, and it tells you nothing either.

11

What AI gets wrong in this course

  • Fake sources. It invents citations, authors, and page numbers with total confidence.
  • Loops that are not loops. It produces diagrams with unlabeled arrows, no s / o markings, and no closed loop. Those fail the simple-loop check.
  • Events-level thinking. It tells you what happened and what repeats, then stops. Structures and mental models, the two levels that matter most here, are where it is weakest.
  • Flattened perspectives. It averages people into “students” or “users” and loses who faces which barrier. Week 11 exists precisely to train the opposite habit.
  • Surface fixes. It proposes posters and awareness campaigns when the leverage point is a rule, a flow of information, or who decides.
  • Local facts. It is confidently wrong about NYU Shanghai, campus services, local context, and anything recent.

How this maps to the EAP AI policy

Your syllabus carries the EAP AI Policy Statement, which sets six requirements for any AI use in an EAP course. This guide is how those six requirements work in EAP 101.

EAP policy requirementWhat it means in EAP 101
Critical assessment and citationCite AI output in APA when you quote it. Never cite AI as a source for facts. Every reference in your work must be a real source you have opened and read.
Demonstrate your own understandingI will ask you to explain your own work: in the Week 9 meeting, in both seminars, and in feedback. Your map, your loop, your labels, your sources.
TransparencyThe four-line AI Use Statement at the end of the file. No other format is accepted.
ResponsibilityAn error AI made is your error. A fabricated citation is a fabricated citation, whoever produced it.
Group workFor in-class mapping and seminar groups, agree what you will use before you start, and say what you used.
Ethical useIf you are unsure whether a use is permitted, ask me before you submit, not after.
One difference worth knowing. The EAP policy says AI can be helpful for “brainstorming, drafting, and enhancing your understanding”. In EAP 101, drafting means working on text that you wrote: your outline, your sentences, your structure. It does not mean generating sentences and submitting them. Everything else in this guide follows directly from the policy.

If you are not sure, and what happens if you hide it

Ask before you submit. One email or one minute after class: kt1992@nyu.edu, or Room N745. Asking is never penalized. Guessing often is.

I will ask you to explain your own work. In the Week 9 meeting, in seminars, and in feedback conversations. If you cannot explain a sentence, a label, or a source in your own words, the submission is treated as not your own and the course follows the NYU Shanghai Statement on Academic Integrity.

Honest disclosure is safe. An AI Use Statement describing amber-zone use costs you no marks. Undisclosed AI in a graded submission is the problem, not the tool.
